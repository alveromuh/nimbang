# Bab 8 — Kecerdasan sosial (out of scope)

**Photo files:** p081–p091
**Printed pages seen:** 207–258

## What it establishes

- **Cipolla's four categories** (intelligent, bandit, helpless, stupid), keyed on
  whether an action benefits or harms the actor and others. *(p081, p086)*
- **Incentives and disincentives** as levers to change other people's behaviour,
  with examples about corruption and theft prevention. *(p082–p083)*
- **An "integrated CBA"**: score benefit and cost for yourself *and* for others,
  then classify the decision by which side it helps. *(p085–p086)*
- A caution against assuming malice where incompetence explains the behaviour.
  *(p084)*

## Why this is out of scope

1. **Cipolla is satire.** It was written as a joke about human folly, not as a
   measurement instrument. Building a "who is stupid" classifier into a decision
   tool would be both unserious and socially hostile.
2. **The integrated CBA cannot be scored honestly.** "Benefit to others" has no
   single scale, and the app would be inviting users to assign numbers to other
   people's welfare and then pronounce on it. That is a different product with a
   different burden of justification.
3. **Incentive design is a real discipline, but it answers a different
   question.** It asks "how do I get others to act", not "what should I choose".
   That is a mechanism-design tool, not a decision tool.

## What is salvageable, if anything

One idea, and it does not need the rest: **whose preference is this?** A cost or
benefit item sometimes belongs to someone else (a parent, a partner, an
employer). Tagging an item with whose value it is prevents the user from
silently scoring other people's preferences as their own.

That is already covered by the preference-tagging in Bab 2, so nothing new is
needed.
