# Bab 1 — Peta realitas dan tujuan

**Photo files:** p002–p022 (order does not follow the book; page numbers as printed)
**Printed pages seen:** 6–57

## What it establishes

- **Goal is prior to problem.** A situation is only a "problem" relative to a
  goal. The book's example: a broken commuter line is a problem for someone
  travelling Depok→Bogor and not for someone flying Jakarta→Singapore. Same
  fact, different goal, different problem. *(p033)*
- **Reality map = cause-and-effect chains.** Decisions are only as good as the
  accuracy of the causal model behind them. *(p017–p018)*
- **Two ingredients of a decision:** information (the map) and reasoning (how
  you read it). *(p021)*
- **Deliberate vs emergent goals.** A goal can be chosen up front, or allowed to
  emerge from action. The book treats both as legitimate. *(p011–p013)*
- **Herzberg two-factor framing.** Hygiene factors reduce dissatisfaction;
  motivation factors produce satisfaction. Applied to life goals: removing what
  you hate is not the same as pursuing what you want. *(p008–p010)*

## Maps to our framework

- Stage **Frame**: objectives before options. This is the book's version of
  Keeney's value-focused thinking.
- Stage **Frame**, sub-question: is this objective a hygiene goal (remove a
  pain) or a motivation goal (reach a good)? Worth one radio button, because
  users routinely state hygiene goals as if they were aims.

## Primary source

Keeney, *Value-Focused Thinking* (1992) — objectives before alternatives.
Herzberg, *The Motivation to Work* (1959) — two-factor theory.

## Do not copy

The commuter-line example, the "reality map" name, the fruit/tree metaphor
(p007), the authors' personal anecdotes (p014). Use our own examples.
